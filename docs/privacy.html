Privacy Policy

Effective 4 October 2026

Daybook is a private journal. Your writing is yours. This page says plainly what is stored, where, who can see it, and how to remove it.

Who runs Daybook

Daybook is made and run by Geo Subash, an individual developer. Questions about privacy: geosubash@gmail.com.

What is stored

  • Your account: the email address and name on the Google account you sign in with (or the email you registered), and an account ID.
  • Your journal: each entry's date, title and text.
  • Your settings: a scrambled check of your passcode (never the passcode itself), and your auto-correct choice.

Daybook does not use advertising, does not sell or share your data, and does not track you across other apps or sites. It has no analytics.

Where it is stored

Your journal is stored in Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore), in a private area that only your account can read or write. A copy is also kept on your device so you can write offline. It uploads when you reconnect.

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub receives ordinary web requests, such as your IP address, when you open the site.

Who can see your writing

Only you, through your signed-in account. The database rules refuse every other account. Be aware of two honest limits: your entries are protected by sign-in and by Google's encryption in transit and at rest, but they are not end-to-end encrypted, so the person who administers the Firebase project could technically open the database in the Firebase console. That person is Geo Subash, who does not read journals and will only access data if you ask for help or the law requires it. The passcode inside Daybook is a screen lock, not encryption.

A feature that sends text for correction

If you use Correct grammar or Auto-correct, the text being checked (the whole entry, or the paragraph you are writing) is sent to LanguageTool's free checking service, which returns suggested fixes. LanguageTool says it does not store the text it checks. Nothing else, and nothing about your account, is sent. Both features are optional and off unless you press them, and Daybook asks you to confirm before it sends text the first time.

Anonymous app health data

To fix crashes and slow starts, Daybook sends small technical reports: the kind of event (app opened, error), how many milliseconds the app took to open, the error message and the file it came from, the app version, your Android version and phone model, and a random install ID that is not linked to your account. It never includes your entries, titles, photos, passcode, email or name. Turn it off any time in Settings under Anonymous app health. These reports are stored in the same Firebase project and are used only to improve the app.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it. Deleting an entry removes it. Deleting your account removes everything.

Your choices and rights

  • See and export: use Print or save for a text or printable copy, and Settings, then Back up everything, for a complete file.
  • Delete everything: Settings, then Delete my account and journal. It erases all entries, settings and your sign-in at once. You can also read how to request deletion.
  • Anything else that your local law gives you, such as correction or a copy of your data: email the address above.

Children

Daybook is for people aged 13 or over, or the minimum age to use a Google account in your country. It is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top will change and the app will say so.
